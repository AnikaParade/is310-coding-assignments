\## About the Cultural Object



\*Stories to Read or Tell from Fairy Tales and Folklore\* was edited by \*\*Laure Claire Foucher\*\* and published in New York by \*\*Moffat, Yard and Company in 1911\*\*.



The Library of Congress categorizes the book under \*\*Children's Stories, Folklore, and Storytelling\*\*. The digitized version contains 166 pages and illustrations. The Library of Congress makes the book available as online text, images, and a PDF.



The book is particularly relevant to fairy-tale retellings because it collects and presents traditional stories in a form intended to be read or told to an audience. Looking at the book as a digitized cultural object also shows how older versions of fairy tales can be preserved and made available for people to study and reinterpret.

\## Why This Website Is Relevant to Fairy-Tale Retellings



The Library of Congress is useful for studying fairy-tale retellings because it preserves older versions of stories and makes them available as digital cultural objects.



The 1911 book provides an example of how traditional fairy tales and folklore were selected, edited, illustrated, published, and presented to readers in a particular historical period.



Digitization changes the way this cultural object can be accessed. Instead of needing to find the original physical book in a library, a researcher can view scanned pages online, search the text, examine metadata, and download digital versions.



This makes it possible to compare older versions of fairy tales with later retellings and adaptations.
